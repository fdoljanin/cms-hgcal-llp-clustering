\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The year 2030 will mark the start of the High-Luminosity phase of the Large
Hadron Collider (HL-LHC)~\cite{HLLHC}, which will deliver proton--proton
collisions at an unprecedented luminosity. The resulting data
volume and the severe pileup---of order $200$ simultaneous interactions per
bunch crossing---demand a new generation of reconstruction algorithms. To cope
with these conditions the CMS experiment will replace its endcap calorimeters
with the High-Granularity Calorimeter (HGCAL)~\cite{HGCALTDR}, a sampling
calorimeter with roughly six million channels arranged in 47
longitudinal layers. Its fine transverse and longitudinal segmentation capture detailed three-dimensional structure of electromagnetic and hadronic
showers, while also providing energy capture timing information.

The Clustering of Energy (CLUE) algorithm~\cite{Rovere2020clue} is a fast, parallel, energy density-based clustering algorithm that groups HGCAL energy deposits within each layer. In the CMS reconstruction, the Iterative Clustering (TICL)
framework~\cite{TICL} builds objects in stages (Fig.~\ref{fig:rechits}): individual calorimeter hits (\emph{RecHits}) are first
grouped per layer by CLUE into two-dimensional \emph{LayerClusters} (LCs). A
three-dimensional extension of CLUE, \cluethreed{}, then links LayerClusters across
multiple layers into \emph{Tracksters}; a subsequent linking step associates
Tracksters into the final TICL candidates. The Monte Carlo truth is represented
by \emph{SimTracksters}, which collect the LayerClusters associated with a
simulated shower.
Reconstruction performance is measured by comparing reconstructed Tracksters to
SimTracksters through their shared energy.

An important \cluethreed{} assumption: the neighbour search that links layer
clusters across layers is performed in a coordinate system that projects layers towards the interaction point (IP). Consequently, a shower
travelling outward from the CMS centre has a fixed projected position from
layer to layer. This
holds for \emph{prompt} particles produced at the IP, but it doesn't need to for
the class of \emph{long-lived particles} (LLPs) predicted by many extensions of
the Standard Model~\cite{LLPreview}. Rather than decaying within a few
millimetres of IP, an LLP can travel longer distance in the detector before decaying, producing \emph{displaced}, non-pointing
particles. A representative signature is a dark shower in which
long-lived dark hadrons decay to Standard Model photons far from the beamline. Because these showers do not point back to
the origin, they may violate exactly the assumption on which \cluethreed{}
relies.

The goal of this project is to determine
if \cluethreed{} is capable of clustering the energy deposits from such
novel topologies, to measure the expected performance degradation as a function
of the distance from IP to where LLP decays, and to explore an alternative algorithm. We restrict the study to
 \emph{photons}: neutral particles whose trajectory is not curved by the CMS magnetic field, so
they go in straight lines and their displacement is unambiguously defined. We also produce them immediately in front of HGCAL so they also do not interact in the tracker. This isolates the calorimeter clustering behaviour
from tracking and magnetic-field effects and provides the cleanest possible test
of the origin assumption.

The remainder of this report is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:displacement}
defines the displacement observables for reconstructed objects, while
Sections~\ref{sec:gun} and~\ref{sec:metrics} describe the displaced-photon Monte
Carlo production and performance metrics. Section~\ref{sec:clue3d} reviews how
\cluethreed{} works, with emphasis on the origin assumption. We then present
the single-photon efficiency (Section~\ref{sec:singlephoton}), two-shower
merging behaviour (Section~\ref{sec:merging}), and performance under pileup
(Section~\ref{sec:pileup}), before comparing \cluethreed{} with the alternative
algorithm \cluestering{} (Section~\ref{sec:cluestering}) and concluding in
Section~\ref{sec:conclusions}.
