\section{Displaced photon gun}
\label{sec:gun}

As mentioned before, we chose to study displaced photons due to their shower simplicity and straight trajectory. The original displaced-particle gun was already developed for the project (CMSSW pull request
\href{https://github.com/cms-sw/cmssw/pull/51006}{\#51006}); my contribution was
to test and improve it---modifying its sampling and configuration, correcting the
production timing, and updating the beamspot choice---done in pull request
\#51489~\cite{DoljaninPR51489}.

Photons are produced immediately in front of HGCAL, so they do not convert or shower
in the tracker. This isolates energy response to HGCAL. The gun is a controlled test of the reconstruction geometry, not a simulation of
a full LLP production and decay.

\subsection{Trajectory geometry}

The gun samples the origin in the plane $z=0$,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}_0=(\R\cos\phi_0,\R\sin\phi_0,0),
\end{equation}
so its radius is exactly the displacement observable of
Section~\ref{sec:displacement}. A straight trajectory of polar angle $\theta$
and azimuth $\phi_p$ starts from this origin, and the photon is created
where the line crosses a \emph{production plane} near the HGCAL front face
($z\simeq\SI{319}{cm}$); an optional \emph{target plane} near the back of the
electromagnetic calorimeter can constrain a second annular acceptance so that the
trajectory remains within the calorimeter volume. This helps ensure transverse
shower containment, so measurements of reconstruction performance are not
affected by energy leakage. Longitudinal containment is
ensured by the calorimeter depth in radiation lengths.

To keep the requested displacement distribution unbiased, the gun holds the
sampled origin fixed and after choosing $\R$ it constructs the allowed polar-angle intervals
analytically before sampling $\theta$. It samples $\R$ uniformly, giving equal populations per
displacement bin, and offers either a uniform total-energy or a uniform
transverse-momentum distribution. Sampling $E$ directly keeps the energy
distribution independent of the accepted direction, which is useful when
isolating displacement effects.

\subsection{Timing, energy and beamspot corrections}

Validating the pointing limit $\R=0$ against the existing CloseBy gun exposed three issues, all now fixed. First, the original gun assigned $t=0$ to a photon created in front of
HGCAL, ignoring the flight time required to reach that point. Its signals
therefore arrived about $\SI{11}{ns}$ earlier than a particle travelling at the
speed of light from the IP could reach the calorimeter. These arrival times are
incompatible with the timing assumptions of hit reconstruction, so such
signals could be rejected. The resulting reconstructed energy was reduced and
fragmented across the shower, producing several Tracksters even at $\R=0$.
This was therefore a consequence of the incorrect generation time, rather than
a displacement-caused clustering failure. The corrected version assigns the
physical flight time,
\begin{equation}
  ct_{\mathrm{prod}}=|\mathbf{x}_0|
       +\frac{|\mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{prod}}-\mathbf{x}_0|}{\beta},
  \qquad \beta=\frac{|\mathbf{p}|}{E}.
  \label{eq:guntime}
\end{equation}
For photons, $\beta=1$, so at $\R=0$ this reduces to the ordinary
$\sim\SI{11}{ns}$ flight time to the production plane.
Second, the gun sampled $p_T$ rather than energy, which produced much higher
energies at small momentum $\theta$ ($E=p_T/\sin\theta$); direct energy sampling
was added. Third, the workflow used the
\texttt{DBrealisticHLLHC} beamspot smearing and was switched to the fixed \texttt{CloseBy} production.
Together these corrections reproduce the CloseBy response at $\R=0$.
