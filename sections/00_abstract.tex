Long-lived particles can decay far from the beamline, producing photons whose
trajectories do not point back to the interaction point. This breaks the
assumption behind \cluethreed{}, the CMS clustering algorithm for the
High-Granularity Calorimeter, which groups energy along directions projected
from the origin. Using controlled single- and two-photon simulations, with and
without pileup, I study how well such displaced photons are reconstructed as a
function of their transverse displacement.

I first explain geometrically why default \cluethreed{} loses displaced
photons: its origin projection makes points on a single inclined shower appear
transversely separated, so the shower fragments as displacement grows, while in
two-photon events nearby showers merge. The main result is that alternative
\cluestering{} metrics can do better, recovering displaced photons more
completely while keeping neighbouring showers apart. Tuning of the coordinates,
distance metrics and density settings is ongoing, but the results already point
to a clustering better suited to non-pointing photons than the origin-projected
default.
