\section{Reconstruction performance metrics}
\label{sec:metrics}

The Trackster multiplicity of Section~\ref{sec:gun} is a useful single-photon
diagnostic, but it says nothing about how the shower energy is distributed among
reconstructed objects, nor about contamination when several showers overlap. We
therefore use the HGCAL validation framework, which associates reconstructed
Tracksters to SimTracksters by shared calorimeter content and from that defines
efficiency, duplication, merging and fake rates.

\subsection{Shared energy and association scores}

The association uses common LayerCluster content, not proximity of the
reconstructed and simulated axes. Let $e_i^S$ and $e_i^T$ be the energies of
LayerCluster $i$ in a SimTrackster $S$ and a reconstructed Trackster $T$; truth
assignments may be fractional, whereas in \cluethreed{} a LayerCluster is never
shared between Tracksters, so $e_i^T$ is either zero or the full LayerCluster
energy. The shared energy and the recovered fraction are
\begin{equation}
  E_{\mathrm{shared}}(S,T)=\sum_i\min(e_i^S,e_i^T),
  \label{eq:sharedenergy}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  f_{ST}=\frac{E_{\mathrm{shared}}(S,T)}{E_S},
  \qquad E_S=\sum_i e_i^S,
  \label{eq:sharedfraction}
\end{equation}
so $f_{ST}$ measures how much of the true shower---referred to the SimTrackster
energy $E_S$, not the generated photon energy---is collected in a single
reconstructed object. Two directional scores quantify the mismatch in each
direction,
\begin{align}
  s_{S\to T}&=\frac{\sum_{i\in S}\bigl[\max(0,e_i^S-e_i^T)\bigr]^2}
                        {\sum_{i\in S}(e_i^S)^2},
  \label{eq:simrecoscore}\\
  s_{T\to S}&=\frac{\sum_{i\in T}\bigl[\max(0,e_i^T-e_i^S)\bigr]^2}
                        {\sum_{i\in T}(e_i^T)^2}.
  \label{eq:recosimscore}
\end{align}
Lower is better: $s_{S\to T}$ penalizes missing SimTrackster content, while
$s_{T\to S}$ penalizes reconstructed content foreign to $S$. Because they weight
squared energies, a score cut is not equivalent to a cut on $f_{ST}$.

\subsection{Efficiency, duplicate rate and merge rate}

A SimTrackster is counted as reconstructed if some Trackster holds more than half
of its energy. In a bin $B$ of a truth observable,
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon(B)=\frac{N_S(B;\ \exists T:\ f_{ST}>0.5)}{N_S(B)}.
  \label{eq:efficiency}
\end{equation}
Each SimTrackster counts at most once and its fragments are not summed, so
efficiency measures energy recovery in one object, not that the shower is
reconstructed as a single piece. The duplicate rate counts showers reconstructed
more than once, and the merge rate---defined from the reconstructed side---counts
objects matching more than one truth shower,
\begin{equation}
  D(B)=\frac{N_S(B;\ \#\{T:s_{S\to T}<0.6\}\geq2)}{N_S(B)},
  \label{eq:duplicaterate}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  M(B)=\frac{N_T(B;\ \#\{S:s_{T\to S}<0.6\}\geq2)}{N_T(B)},
  \label{eq:mergerate}
\end{equation}
where $N_T(B)$ counts the nonempty reconstructed Tracksters. All three use the
working score threshold $0.6$, an explicit override of the validation default
$0.2$. Small fragments can fail the association cut, so a low duplicate or merge
rate does not by itself exclude fragmentation---a caveat revisited in the
two-shower study.

\subsection{Fake rate, purity and displacement binning}

A reconstructed Trackster is non-fake if it has at least one SimTrackster match
with $s_{T\to S}<0.6$; the fake rate is the complementary fraction, and stays near
zero even for fragments fully attributable to one shower. The validation's
\emph{purity} is instead a truth-side rate---the fraction of SimTracksters with a
match at $s_{S\to T}<0.2$---and should not be confused with the per-object energy
fraction $E_{\mathrm{shared}}/E_T$.

Efficiency, duplicate and purity rates use the SimTrackster denominator and are
binned in truth $\R$ or $\alphainc$; fake and merge rates use the
reconstructed-Trackster denominator and the barycentre-plus-clean-PCA
displacement of Section~\ref{sec:displacement}, with no truth match required, so
they inherit its resolution. Under pileup a signal-only rate counts only the
signal photon's SimTrackster, whereas an inclusive rate also counts pileup
SimTracksters; the sample and truth selection must therefore be stated with every
curve.

\subsection{Improvements to the validation framework}

Applying these metrics to isolated displaced showers required changes to the
standard validation. A restrictive sim-to-reco threshold can hide splitting,
because two fragments need not each resemble the full shower closely enough to
pass. I therefore made the non-fake, merge, purity and duplicate score thresholds
independently configurable, so the duplicate threshold can be relaxed without
touching the energy-based efficiency definition. Together with the displacement
and timing observables and a ROOT segmentation-fault fix in separate-plot
production, these changes went into CMSSW pull request
\href{https://github.com/cms-sw/cmssw/pull/51510}{\#51510}.

A subsequent refactoring of histogram booking and filling (pull request
\href{https://github.com/cms-sw/cmssw/pull/51565}{\#51565}) extended the
validation to two-dimensional distributions. Recording numerator and denominator
jointly in, for example, $\R$ and energy or $\R$ and pseudorapidity allows
performance to be evaluated in slices of the second variable---essential for
distinguishing a true displacement dependence from changes in the energy or
angular composition of the sample.
