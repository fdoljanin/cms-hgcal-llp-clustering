\section{Displacement observables}
\label{sec:displacement}

Every result in this report is expressed as a function of how \emph{displaced}
a shower is. We need to define displacement observables and explain how they are calculated for reconstructed objects that
carry no truth information.

\subsection{Definitions}

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/hgcal_nonpointing.png}
  \caption{Displacement geometry in the $(z,R)$ plane. Pointing photons (red)
  travel radially from the interaction point with $\alphainc=0$; displaced
  photons (green) originate at a transverse distance $\R$ from the beamline at
  $z=0$ and hit the calorimeter with $\alphainc>0$. The two green
  trajectories show that a single $\R$ can give different angles $\alphainc$.
  Figure from Ref.~\cite{Alves2026LLPHGCAL}.}
  \label{fig:geometry}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/CMS-TDR-019_Figure-1.6_HGCAL-longitudinal-cross-section.png}
  \caption{Longitudinal cross section of the upper half of one HGCAL endcap,
  showing the electromagnetic (CE-E) and hadronic (CE-H) compartments.
  Figure from Ref.~\cite{HGCALTDR}, Fig.~1.6.}
  \label{fig:hgcal-layout}
\end{figure}

Consider a photon that is created at
$\mathbf{x}_v=(x_v,y_v,z_v)$ and travels in a straight line to the HGCAL front
face, located at $z\simeq\SI{321}{cm}$ from the interaction
point (Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry}). HGCAL has two endcaps; in this report,
we consider the positive-$z$ endcap. Its layout is illustrated in
Fig.~\ref{fig:hgcal-layout}. We measure the photon's displacement by two
quantities:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the \textbf{displacement radius} \R{}, defined as the transverse
  distance from beamline at which the photon's extrapolated trajectory crosses $z=0$ plane,
  \begin{equation}
    \R = \sqrt{x_0^2 + y_0^2}\,,
    \label{eq:Rdef}
  \end{equation}
  where $(x_0,y_0)$ is extrapolated trajectory to $z=0$;
  \item the \textbf{displacement angle} \alphainc{}, the angle between the
  photon's momentum direction $\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ and the unit vector
  $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{\text{face}}$ from the IP to its entry point on the HGCAL
  front face,
  \begin{equation}
    \alphainc = \arccos\!\left(
      \hat{\mathbf{r}}_{\text{face}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{p}}
    \right).
    \label{eq:alphadef}
  \end{equation}
  A prompt photon has
  $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{\text{face}}\parallel\hat{\mathbf{p}}$ and
  $\R=\alphainc=0$; the non-pointing photons studied here have $\R>0$ and
  $\alphainc>0$.
\end{itemize}
For a truth photon these quantities are trivial, since the
production vertex and momentum are known directly from the generator truth.

\subsection{Reconstructing the displacement}

The difficulty is on the reconstruction side. A reconstructed Trackster has no
persisted vertex where it was produced, and it does not need to correspond one-to-one to a SimTrackster: it
may contain only a fragment of a true shower, or it may merge multiple showers.
The merge-rate metrics defined below require a
displacement value. We therefore reconstruct \R{} directly from the reconstructed
Trackster geometry.

Each Trackster provides an energy-weighted barycentre
$\mathbf{x}_c=(x_c,y_c,z_c)$ and a principal-axis direction
$\mathbf{u}=(u_x,u_y,u_z)$ calculated by principal-component analysis (PCA) of
its LayerClusters. Treating the shower as a straight line through
$\mathbf{x}_c$ with direction $\mathbf{u}$ and extrapolating to $z=0$ gives
\begin{align}
  x_0 &= x_c - z_c\,\frac{u_x}{u_z}\,, &
  y_0 &= y_c - z_c\,\frac{u_y}{u_z}\,,
  \label{eq:recoR}
\end{align}
and $\R_{\text{reco}}=\sqrt{x_0^2+y_0^2}$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:Rdef}. This straight-line treatment is correct for photons, which
makes them the ideal probe for validating performance.

\subsection{Estimate error and the choice of direction estimator}

The accuracy of $\R_{\text{reco}}$ sets resolution for reco-based metrics, so we measured its error,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\R \equiv \R_{\text{reco}} - \R_{\text{truth}},
  \label{eq:deltaR}
\end{equation}
summarized by its RMS. Starting from the ordinary PCA that CMSSW
already exposes, the error is broad,
$\text{RMS}(\Delta\R)\approx\SI{20}{cm}$. This error has only two possible
sources, the barycentre and the direction, and separating them shows that it
comes almost entirely from the \emph{direction}: reconstructing only
barycentre and using the true direction gives $\SI{1.1}{cm}$, and
reconstructing only direction and using the true barycentre gives $\sim\SI{20}{cm}$. It is because the barycentre sits inside the
shower and is well measured, but the small angular uncertainty of the direction is
amplified over $z_c\sim\SI{320}{cm}$ extrapolation back to $z=0$.

\begin{figure}[!tp]
  \centering
  \cmsplot[width=\linewidth]{figures/r_resolution_methods_leading.png}
  \caption{Error distribution $\Delta\R$ for each direction estimator,
  restricted to the leading Trackster per event. The PCA
  variants are the narrowest (clean PCA \SI{9.91}{cm}, ordinary \SI{9.98}{cm}).}
  \label{fig:methods}
\end{figure}

Since the direction estimate dominates the error, we tested several direction
estimators:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Ordinary PCA}: the CMSSW default, using all LayerClusters.
  \item \textbf{Clean PCA}: PCA using only the leading
  LayerCluster per layer in a window 10 layers before after around the shower maximum.
  \item \textbf{Endpoint layers}: the direction between the first and last
  populated layers in the window.
  \item \textbf{Window barycentres}: an axis constructed from per-layer
  energy-weighted barycentres.
  \item \textbf{Weighted straight-line fit}: an energy-weighted fit to the
  LayerClusters.
  \item \textbf{Weighted line fit with outlier refit}: the same fit after
  removing the LayerClusters with the largest residuals.
  \item \textbf{RecHit PCA}: PCA performed on raw RecHits rather than
  LayerClusters.
\end{itemize}
Figure~\ref{fig:methods} compares these methods for the leading Trackster per
event. The PCA variants are already the narrowest; because clean PCA is robust
and implemented in CMSSW, it was adopted.
Window barycentres may be faster to compute than PCA, but runtime benchmarks
would be needed to compare the methods at similar spatial resolution.

The resolution improves with Trackster energy, as a more energetic shower gives
the PCA more LayerClusters to fit its axis; $\sim\SI{10}{cm}$ is
leading-Trackster resolution.

The displacement observables \R{} and \alphainc{}, together with a
Trackster-timing observable, were added to the standard HGCAL validation as new
per-object quantities so that any efficiency, purity, duplicate, fake or merge plot
can be produced as a function of displacement (CMSSW pull request
\#51510~\cite{DoljaninPR51510}).
